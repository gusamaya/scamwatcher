Scam library

Scams we see across Australia

Scammers copy the organisations people trust most: banks, the ATO, toll roads, delivery companies and family. Here's what to watch for.

Tolls and fines

Unpaid toll or fine

A text says you owe a toll or traffic fine and must pay today through a link, or face extra fees.

Safe move: Check your toll or fines account by typing the official website address yourself.

Delivery

Parcel on hold

A message says a parcel can't be delivered and asks for a small "redelivery" fee or your address details.

Safe move: Track parcels with the tracking number in the official courier app.

Government

ATO and myGov

An email or text says you have a tax refund, a debt, or a myGov problem, with a link to sign in.

Safe move: Sign in by typing my.gov.au yourself. Don't use links in messages.

Banking

Bank security alerts

A message says a payment is "on hold" or your account is locked, and asks you to confirm details or call a number.

Safe move: Call the number on the back of your card, or use your bank's app.

Business

Fake invoices and changed bank details

An invoice or email from a "supplier" says their bank account has changed and asks you to pay the new one.

Safe move: Confirm any change of bank details by phone, using a number you already have.

Investment

Investment and crypto offers

Ads or messages promising big, fast returns, often with fake celebrity endorsements or a "trading platform".

Safe move: Check the company on ASIC's Moneysmart before you invest anything.

Work

Job and task scams

A message offers easy online work rating products or apps, then asks you to pay in to "unlock" your earnings.

Safe move: Real jobs don't ask you to pay to start or to get paid.

Family

"Hi Mum" messages

A message from a new number says it's your child or relative with a new phone, and they need money urgently.

Safe move: Call them on their old number before you send anything.

Accounts

Account takeover

Messages saying your Facebook, email or Netflix account will be closed, or asking you to send back a code sent to your phone.

Safe move: Never share a code sent to your phone. Check accounts in the official app.

Act quickly

Already paid, clicked or shared details?

It happens to careful people too. You have nothing to be ashamed of. The faster you act, the better the chance of stopping further loss.

    Change your passwords.

Who we are

Helping people and communities make safer digital decisions.

ScamWatcher is an independent advisory service, built in Australia, that helps individuals, families, community groups and organisations recognise scams before they act.

Our free assessment service looks for the warning signs in suspicious messages and gives clear, practical guidance before people respond, share personal information or make a payment.
